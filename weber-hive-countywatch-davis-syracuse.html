Two Deals to Watch in Syracuse

A Public Infrastructure District borrows against your school district to build Costco's parking lot. Meanwhile, a gym developer wants a bigger tax rebate after missing his own contract's specs.

The Weber County Hive · Updated Aug. 29, 2026

This page uses a lot of acronyms. Tap a card to flip it →

01

CRA

↻ Tap

Community Reinvestment Area. A zone a city draws around a property so it can capture the growth in property tax that new development creates there, instead of that growth going to the county, school district, etc. like normal.

02

PID

↻ Tap

Public Infrastructure District. A separate mini-government, created just for one project, that borrows money (a bond) to build roads/sewer/etc. and pays it back using the CRA's redirected tax money.

03

RDA

↻ Tap

Redevelopment Agency. The city body — usually the same Mayor and Council, just wearing a different legal hat — that actually creates the CRA and collects the redirected tax money on the city's behalf.

04

Tax Increment (TIF)

↻ Tap

The growth in property tax value above a fixed starting point (the "base"). Only this growth gets redirected — everyone still collects full tax on the base value the whole time.

05

Base Taxable Value

↻ Tap

The property's value on record the year the CRA is created — the starting line. Tax on this amount always keeps flowing normally to the county, school district, etc.

06

Bond & Trustee

↻ Tap

A bond is the loan the PID takes out to build infrastructure. A trustee is a board member who governs the PID and decides how that money gets spent.

07

Interlocal Agreement

↻ Tap

The actual contract between two government bodies — here, between the RDA and each taxing entity (or the RDA and the PID) — spelling out exactly how much money moves, and for how long.

08

NPV

↻ Tap

Net Present Value. A dollar promised 20 years from now is worth less than a dollar today. NPV converts a long list of future payments into what they're actually worth in today's money.

09

Lease Revenue Bond

↻ Tap

How a school board can borrow to build without a public vote — by creating a "Local Building Authority" (itself, wearing a second hat), which issues the bond and leases the building back to the district. A normal school bond requires voter approval; this doesn't.

Deal one

Syracuse WDC Gateway: Costco, a PID, and a CRA

168.89 acres at Antelope Drive and 3000 West, right off the new West Davis Corridor exit. The City rezoned it, created a tax-increment district over it, and stood up a public infrastructure district specifically so Costco could build the roads.

THE STRUCTURE

Three entities, one project

Syracuse City Redevelopment Agency — created the Community Reinvestment Area (CRA), captures the tax increment.
Gateway Public Infrastructure District No. 1 — a separate political subdivision, incorporated Aug. 26, 2024, issues the bonds.
Costco Wholesale Corporation and the D. Lawrence Cook Family Trust — jointly defined as "Developer" in the interlocal agreement. Costco requested the District's creation.

One vote against, every time

Council minutes for Aug. 13, 2024 — the night the PID, the CRA Plan/Budget, and the interlocal agreement were all first created — show Councilmember Carver voting no on all three, every other member voting yes. She was still the sole dissenting vote when the final interlocal agreement came back for approval in April 2025 — the only Council or RDA member to oppose this deal at any point across its documented history.

The public hearing drew no public

Utah law requires a public hearing before a PID can be created. Minutes record it plainly: "Mayor Maughan opened the public hearing at 9:10 p.m. There were no persons appearing to be heard and the public hearing was closed." The item came immediately after that night's Truth in Taxation hearing — the one guaranteed forum where residents typically show up to discuss taxes.

Davis School District's individual share — $15.66M nominal, $7.35M in today's dollars — is close to the entire $15M cap the CRA has agreed to pay the PID toward Costco's bond. The school district alone is carrying nearly the whole infrastructure bill, even before Syracuse City, Davis County, and the other three entities add theirs on top.

A pattern, not a one-off

At the combined tax rate this same Budget documents (1.0273%), that $219M in already-diverted incremental value works out to roughly $2.25 million a year kept out of the county, school district, and other taxing entities' reach — across four zones that predate WDC Gateway. Add WDC Gateway's own hundreds of millions in projected value and this is at least five overlapping tax-increment districts running simultaneously in one small city.

ADMINISTRATIVE COSTS, PER THE BUDGET

Who gets paid to run this

3% of increment is set aside for Syracuse's own administrative costs — an estimated ~$775,000 to the city over 25 years. Davis County requires the same percentage be paid to it based on the County's own contribution — an estimated ~$87,000 to Davis County over the same period.

Where the district turns instead

Davis School District already borrows without a vote

Not because of this deal specifically — but the same underlying pressure (growth outpacing revenue) shows up in both places.

STANDARD-EXAMINER, MAY 2022

$40 million, West Point Elementary, no public vote

The school board — acting as the district's own Local Building Authority — approved a $40M lease revenue bond to relieve overcrowding at West Point Elementary. District spokesperson Christopher Williams confirmed it was the first time the district had ever used this mechanism. A normal school bond needs voter approval; this route only needs a board vote and a public hearing, unless residents petition to force a ballot question.

The cap is gone now, too

Utah used to limit lease revenue bonds to $200 million combined per any 3-year period without a vote. S.B. 188 (2025) removed that cap for reorganized districts. The first to use the expanded authority: Alpine School District, approving up to $238M for two schools and bringing $201M to market days later. Those bonds run up to 31 years. A comparable Davis-area example, North Summit School District, sought up to $125 million this way in December 2025 — its own public notice disclosing that the project's original bond had already been rejected by voters, and that lease revenue bonds generally cost more in interest than the voter-approved kind.

Two ends of the same pipe

This isn't a claim that the WDC Gateway CRA caused the 2022 West Point bond — it predates the CRA by two years. It's a structural point: the same district now giving up $15.66M in future growth to this one CRA has a documented history of turning to no-vote financing specifically when growth outpaces its ordinary revenue. Davis still uses traditional voter-approved bonds for its largest asks (a $475M bond in 2022, $298M in 2015) — but when a smaller, faster need shows up, the no-vote tool is the one on record getting used.

Meanwhile, county-wide

Davis County just raised taxes for the first time in nine years

The county's own stated reason for the hike is, almost word for word, the mechanism this whole page has been walking through.

DEC. 2, 2025 — FIRST INCREASE SINCE 2016

14.9%, after a proposed 30%

Commissioners proposed up to a 30% property tax hike in October 2025 ($12.68M). After a Truth in Taxation hearing, the Commission voted 2-1 (Kamalu and Stevenson yes, Crofts dissenting) for a lower 14.9% increase instead — about $6 million, roughly $4/month on the average home. It was the county's first tax increase since 2016.

The county's own words

From Davis County's own Truth in Taxation materials: "Counties in Utah do not receive more property tax revenue simply because property values rise. Revenue changes only when the certified tax rate is adjusted." And separately: "Costs have grown nearly twice as fast as revenue." That's the county stating, in its own words, that ordinary growth wasn't generating enough new tax revenue to keep pace with what growth costs.

A juxtaposition, not a proven cause

Nothing in the county's public materials names WDC Gateway or any specific CRA as a reason for the increase — that link isn't documented and shouldn't be overstated. What is documented: the county cited a structural gap between revenue and costs as its reason for the first tax hike in nine years, in the same window that Syracuse alone was running five simultaneous tax-increment zones worth $219 million+ in diverted incremental value (see "The bigger picture," above). Every dollar of new growth walled off in a CRA is a dollar that doesn't show up in the county's own rate calculation — worth knowing, even without a document that draws the line explicitly.

ONE MORE THING WORTH KNOWING

No UIPA project area in Davis County — at least not yet

Utah's Inland Port Authority now runs 16 project areas statewide — Salt Lake, Weber, Box Elder, Tooele (two), Iron, six rural counties via the Agri-Park, Sanpete/Sevier/Wayne, San Juan, Duchesne, and Washington City. Davis County isn't among them. Whatever is happening to Davis County's tax base is happening through ordinary city-level CRAs like this one — plural — not through the state's inland-port apparatus.

What's already been reported — and what hasn't

This isn't uncovered territory. This specific connection is.

Utah's PID debt is a real, actively-reported story. Nobody has connected it to county tax hikes like Davis's — that's the gap this page fills.

WHAT'S ALREADY OUT THERE

Real reporting exists, and it's substantial

The Utah Investigative Journalism Project, in partnership with KSL, has published two major pieces: "Hidden developers' tax is zapping Utah homebuyers for billions" (April 2026) — the story of a St. George homebuyer who found a $10,000/year property tax bill instead of the $2,000 he expected — and "Critics say Utah law gives unelected developers too much taxing power," examining how H.B. 507 lets cities keep PID debt off their own balance sheets. KPCW has covered State Auditor Tina Cannon's warnings repeatedly, including an August 2026 report that the SEC is now scrutinizing Utah's PID municipal bond market directly.

What that coverage focuses on

Every piece found treats two things as the story: what a PID mill levy costs the homebuyer who lives directly inside it, and what the statewide debt total ($5.267B and climbing) means for Utah's credit rating and bond market oversight. Real, important, well-reported.

What none of it does

Connect PID/CRA tax-increment diversion to why county governments raise everyone's general tax rate — PID resident or not. Davis County's own Truth in Taxation materials, and every news article covering its 14.9% hike, cite inflation and staffing costs. None mention that Syracuse alone is running five simultaneous CRAs diverting $219 million+ in growth, or that Weber Basin Water Conservancy District — one of the six entities losing money to WDC Gateway specifically — was separately seeking a tax increase across five counties, Davis included, in the same period.

What the Interlocal Tax Sharing Agreement actually says

Pulled directly from the draft agreement between the Redevelopment Agency and Gateway PID No. 1, dated March 18, 2025.

TermDetail

Base taxable value$505,358 — the site's value as of year-end 2023, before any of this. (Confirmed twice in the official Project Area Budget, July 2024.)
Projected tax base after development~$198 million increase, per the CRA Plan — the number the 50% capture rate gets applied against.
Off the topOf that 50%: 3% to the City for admin, 3% to the County for admin, 10% set aside for affordable housing, and 3% (capped at $500,000) to the Agency for landscaping and public art — before anything reaches the bond.
Payment Period ends at the earliest of:(a) 25 years, (b) $15,000,000 paid to the District, or (c) whatever pays off the bond's principal and interest.
Eligible Public ImprovementsWest New Road A (1,300 ft), South New Road B (1,215 ft), a sanitary sewer extension, turn-lane striping, and right-of-way costs — capped at $6,320,132.
Agreement terminationEarlier of 1 year after the Payment Period ends, or 50 years from signing.
Why this matters: the real capture rate is lower than the headline numbers suggest
  • The $25.8M Budget figure and the 50%-of-increment definition aren't contradictory — the Budget number is a revenue ceiling across the district's full life; the 50% rate governs how fast the district actually gets there each year.
  • After the City/County admin cuts and the 10% housing set-aside, roughly 81% of that 50% slice — call it ~40.5% of the total increment — is what actually reaches the bond repayment in a given year.
  • None of this changes the Davis School District total above; the $15,656,825 figure already reflects the district's full negotiated share under its own separate interlocal agreement.
Who's actually in charge

The PID's board includes the landowner himself

Utah law allows this, and Syracuse's own resolution says so directly.

RESOLUTION R24-31, AUG. 13, 2024

Gateway PID No. 1's initial Board of Trustees

Dale Cook — Trustee, 6-year term
Bill Bockman — Trustee, 6-year term
John Shaw — Trustee, 4-year term

The landowner appointed to govern his own district

Dale Cook appears to be tied to the D. Lawrence Cook Family Trust — the same trust that petitioned for the PID's creation and is named alongside Costco as co-"Developer" in the interlocal agreement. If that's the same person, the landowner who asked for this district sits on the board deciding how its $8.6M bond gets spent. This isn't incidental: the Resolution itself certifies that all three trustees qualify under Utah Code §17D-4-202(c) because they are "agents of property owners within the District's boundaries" — and the Governing Document states any owner of at least one-third of the District's taxable value "shall be entitled to nominate one trustee seat." At this stage, the board is designed to be landowner-controlled. The Governing Document's own annual-report requirements reference future "progress towards milestones required for transition to elected Board" — meaning that transition hasn't happened yet.

100% consent, confirmed

Utah's PID Act requires a petition signed by 100% of surface property owners before a district can be created. Syracuse's own hearing notice confirms it happened here: "consent to the creation of the Proposed District and waiver of the protest period has been obtained from all property owners." With Cook representing the primary landowner, that consent requirement — the same one that shaped how SITLA structured its North Temple Landfill sale — was straightforward to satisfy.

No mill levy — a real correction

The Governing Document states plainly: "The District shall not be permitted to impose ad valorem property taxes for any purpose." Gateway PID No. 1 cannot put a tax line on any homeowner's bill. It runs on Tax Increment Revenue (the CRA diversion above) and one-time Assessments that, for residential parcels, are "payable before transfer of title" — a cost baked into a home's price before it's ever sold, not an annual charge to whoever buys it.

Deal two — same city, unrelated

A gym developer under-built the pool, then asked for a bigger rebate

A separate, much older Davis County tax-increment story — not connected to Costco or the Gateway PID — surfaced in the same Syracuse RDA meeting records.

THE DEAL

Shadow Point, LLC — EOS Fitness gym

Part of the Syracuse Antelope Drive CDA (created 2016). In 2020, the RDA agreed to rebate 75% of the tax increment generated by a planned 40,000+ sq ft gym back to the developer, contingent on the facility meeting specific contractual requirements — including a 25-yard lap pool with three 8-foot lanes, a half-court basketball facility, saunas, a spa, and childcare space.

The compliance problem

City staff inspected the site and found: the pool as permitted is 66 feet (22 yards) long, not the contractually required 75 feet (25 yards) — 9 feet short — and its lanes are 7'4" wide, not the required 8'. Separately, the contract's Nov. 1, 2024 deadline for foundation footings to be complete was missed; staff found footings only about 50% poured on inspection.

In April 2024 — before either shortfall was discovered — the City had already amended the agreement once, raising the developer's rebate from 75% to 100% of the tax increment. Per the City's own staff memo, that increase is worth an estimated $177,517 more to the developer over 15 years (2026–2041) than the original 75% rate would have paid. At the April 8, 2025 RDA special meeting, the developer (Matthew Swain, Perry Commercial) requested the agreement be amended again to formally excuse both shortfalls — the Board voted unanimously to deny the request.

THE VOTE, APRIL 8, 2025

The Board said no

Boardmember Carver moved to deny Swain's request; Watson seconded; all voted in favor. Carver directly disputed the developer's own timeline, noting she joined the Board after COVID and recalls the pool specification being discussed post-pandemic — meaning the pandemic couldn't explain the undersized pool. Savage stated the developer's justification "would not be held up in any court of law if this matter were litigated." Cragun flagged the precedent risk of excusing noncompliance discovered only after a deadline had passed.

Same meeting, same night

Minutes show the Board approved the Interlocal Tax Sharing Agreement with Gateway PID No. 1 immediately before taking up the Shadow Point request — and that vote was not unanimous. Boardmember Carver — the same Council member who opposed the PID, the CRA, and the interlocal authorization back in August 2024 — cast the lone "no" again here, then led the motion to deny Swain's amendment minutes later. Mayor Maughan stated on the record that the interlocal agreement's approval was required before "the PID bond [could] go to the market for sale."

Public offering, or a private sale?

At the Feb. 25, 2025 work session, Carver asked whether citizens could buy into the bond. City Manager Bovero's answer: the City has no control over that — Gateway PID No. 1, as a separate governmental agency, could choose either a public offering or a private placement. Which one it actually became isn't yet confirmed here. A private placement would mean the $8M bond was sold directly to a small number of institutional buyers, with far less public visibility than a public muni-bond sale.

Not the only CRA in Syracuse

A separate rezone item at the same Feb. 25, 2025 meeting references a "2500 W CRA project area" — a third tax-increment district, distinct from both WDC Gateway and the older Antelope Drive CDA covering the Shadow Point/EOS gym. Not yet investigated here, but worth knowing this city is running at least three overlapping CRAs.
