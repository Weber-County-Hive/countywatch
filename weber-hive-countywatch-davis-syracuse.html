Two Deals to Watch in Syracuse

A Public Infrastructure District borrows against your school district to build Costco's parking lot. Meanwhile, a gym developer wants a bigger tax rebate after missing his own contract's specs.

The Weber County Hive · Updated Aug. 29, 2026
Deal one

Syracuse WDC Gateway: Costco, a PID, and a CRA

168.89 acres at Antelope Drive and 3000 West, right off the new West Davis Corridor exit. The City rezoned it, created a tax-increment district over it, and stood up a public infrastructure district specifically so Costco could build the roads.

THE STRUCTURE

Three entities, one project

Syracuse City Redevelopment Agency — created the Community Reinvestment Area (CRA), captures the tax increment.
Gateway Public Infrastructure District No. 1 — a separate political subdivision, incorporated Aug. 26, 2024, issues the bonds.
Costco Wholesale Corporation and the D. Lawrence Cook Family Trust — jointly defined as "Developer" in the interlocal agreement. Costco requested the District's creation.

One vote against, every time

Council minutes for Aug. 13, 2024 — the night the PID, the CRA Plan/Budget, and the interlocal agreement were all first created — show Councilmember Carver voting no on all three, every other member voting yes. She was still the sole dissenting vote when the final interlocal agreement came back for approval in April 2025 — the only Council or RDA member to oppose this deal at any point across its documented history.

The public hearing drew no public

Utah law requires a public hearing before a PID can be created. Minutes record it plainly: "Mayor Maughan opened the public hearing at 9:10 p.m. There were no persons appearing to be heard and the public hearing was closed." The item came immediately after that night's Truth in Taxation hearing — the one guaranteed forum where residents typically show up to discuss taxes.

Worth sitting with

Davis School District's individual share — $15.66M — is close to the entire $15M cap the CRA has agreed to pay the PID toward Costco's bond. The school district alone is carrying nearly the whole infrastructure bill, even before Syracuse City, Davis County, and the other three entities add theirs on top.

Why this matters: the real capture rate is lower than the headline numbers suggest
  • The $25.8M Budget figure and the 50%-of-increment definition aren't contradictory — the Budget number is a revenue ceiling across the district's full life; the 50% rate governs how fast the district actually gets there each year.
  • After the City/County admin cuts and the 10% housing set-aside, roughly 81% of that 50% slice — call it ~40.5% of the total increment — is what actually reaches the bond repayment in a given year.
  • None of this changes the Davis School District total above; the $15,656,825 figure already reflects the district's full negotiated share under its own separate interlocal agreement.
Who's actually in charge

The PID's board includes the landowner himself

Utah law allows this, and Syracuse's own resolution says so directly.

RESOLUTION R24-31, AUG. 13, 2024

Gateway PID No. 1's initial Board of Trustees

Dale Cook — Trustee, 6-year term
Bill Bockman — Trustee, 6-year term
John Shaw — Trustee, 4-year term

The landowner appointed to govern his own district

Dale Cook appears to be tied to the D. Lawrence Cook Family Trust — the same trust that petitioned for the PID's creation and is named alongside Costco as co-"Developer" in the interlocal agreement. If that's the same person, the landowner who asked for this district sits on the board deciding how its $8.6M bond gets spent. This isn't incidental: the Resolution itself certifies that all three trustees qualify under Utah Code §17D-4-202(c) because they are "agents of property owners within the District's boundaries" — and the Governing Document states any owner of at least one-third of the District's taxable value "shall be entitled to nominate one trustee seat." At this stage, the board is designed to be landowner-controlled. The Governing Document's own annual-report requirements reference future "progress towards milestones required for transition to elected Board" — meaning that transition hasn't happened yet.

100% consent, confirmed

Utah's PID Act requires a petition signed by 100% of surface property owners before a district can be created. Syracuse's own hearing notice confirms it happened here: "consent to the creation of the Proposed District and waiver of the protest period has been obtained from all property owners." With Cook representing the primary landowner, that consent requirement — the same one that shaped how SITLA structured its North Temple Landfill sale — was straightforward to satisfy.

No mill levy — a real correction

The Governing Document states plainly: "The District shall not be permitted to impose ad valorem property taxes for any purpose." Gateway PID No. 1 cannot put a tax line on any homeowner's bill. It runs on Tax Increment Revenue (the CRA diversion above) and one-time Assessments that, for residential parcels, are "payable before transfer of title" — a cost baked into a home's price before it's ever sold, not an annual charge to whoever buys it.

Deal two — same city, unrelated

A gym developer under-built the pool, then asked for a bigger rebate

A separate, much older Davis County tax-increment story — not connected to Costco or the Gateway PID — surfaced in the same Syracuse RDA meeting records.

THE DEAL

Shadow Point, LLC — EOS Fitness gym

Part of the Syracuse Antelope Drive CDA (created 2016). In 2020, the RDA agreed to rebate 75% of the tax increment generated by a planned 40,000+ sq ft gym back to the developer, contingent on the facility meeting specific contractual requirements — including a 25-yard lap pool with three 8-foot lanes, a half-court basketball facility, saunas, a spa, and childcare space.

The compliance problem

City staff inspected the site and found: the pool as permitted is 66 feet (22 yards) long, not the contractually required 75 feet (25 yards) — 9 feet short — and its lanes are 7'4" wide, not the required 8'. Separately, the contract's Nov. 1, 2024 deadline for foundation footings to be complete was missed; staff found footings only about 50% poured on inspection.

In April 2024 — before either shortfall was discovered — the City had already amended the agreement once, raising the developer's rebate from 75% to 100% of the tax increment. Per the City's own staff memo, that increase is worth an estimated $177,517 more to the developer over 15 years (2026–2041) than the original 75% rate would have paid. At the April 8, 2025 RDA special meeting, the developer (Matthew Swain, Perry Commercial) requested the agreement be amended again to formally excuse both shortfalls — the Board voted unanimously to deny the request.

THE VOTE, APRIL 8, 2025

The Board said no

Boardmember Carver moved to deny Swain's request; Watson seconded; all voted in favor. Carver directly disputed the developer's own timeline, noting she joined the Board after COVID and recalls the pool specification being discussed post-pandemic — meaning the pandemic couldn't explain the undersized pool. Savage stated the developer's justification "would not be held up in any court of law if this matter were litigated." Cragun flagged the precedent risk of excusing noncompliance discovered only after a deadline had passed.

Same meeting, same night

Minutes show the Board approved the Interlocal Tax Sharing Agreement with Gateway PID No. 1 immediately before taking up the Shadow Point request — and that vote was not unanimous. Boardmember Carver — the same Council member who opposed the PID, the CRA, and the interlocal authorization back in August 2024 — cast the lone "no" again here, then led the motion to deny Swain's amendment minutes later. Mayor Maughan stated on the record that the interlocal agreement's approval was required before "the PID bond [could] go to the market for sale."

Public offering, or a private sale?

At the Feb. 25, 2025 work session, Carver asked whether citizens could buy into the bond. City Manager Bovero's answer: the City has no control over that — Gateway PID No. 1, as a separate governmental agency, could choose either a public offering or a private placement. Which one it actually became isn't yet confirmed here. A private placement would mean the $8M bond was sold directly to a small number of institutional buyers, with far less public visibility than a public muni-bond sale.

Not the only CRA in Syracuse

A separate rezone item at the same Feb. 25, 2025 meeting references a "2500 W CRA project area" — a third tax-increment district, distinct from both WDC Gateway and the older Antelope Drive CDA covering the Shadow Point/EOS gym. Not yet investigated here, but worth knowing this city is running at least three overlapping CRAs.
